01 / OBSERVE

A traceable observation record

The sample workspace uses reproducible synthetic monthly observations. Import prepared observations from your own optical, radar, climate, or field-data workflow to analyze a real region.

Dates, missing values, spatial footprints, and valid-pixel fractions travel with each region. Missing measurements are never described as observed evidence.

02 / DIAGNOSE

A baseline you can inspect

The dashboard uses a transparent vegetation condition index and historical baseline. Its index is a proxy for vegetation condition, not forest survival, remaining area, or a calibrated probability.

Candidate drivers are associations supported by available observations. More than one driver may be plausible; limited evidence can leave the cause uncertain.

03 / INVESTIGATE

Early warning, with limits

Autocorrelation and variance can help investigate resilience. They also respond to seasonality, measurement noise, trends, missing data, and changing external conditions.

Insufficient or irregular histories produce an unavailable result. No output establishes an irreversible tipping point or a time to ecosystem collapse.

Research on remotely sensed resilience ↗
04 / EVALUATE

Research before claims

The existing neural research pipeline supports optical and radar representations, temporal learning, and multi-task training. It is separate from the dashboard’s interpretable baseline.

Pretrained backbones require their documented inputs and actual checkpoint loading. Ecological performance still needs reference labels, spatial and temporal holdouts, and calibration against independent outcomes.

Five-year projections and intervention effects remain research questions. Synthetic training validates the software workflow only.
